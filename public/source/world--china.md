
# China's technology stack

China is the only country building a full technology stack — models, chips, tools, energy and standards — outside the US-led system. The purpose of this brief is not to argue that the stack is equivalent. It is to separate what is shipping from what is announced, because the two are routinely conflated in both optimistic and pessimistic coverage.

## What is actually shipping

The model layer is real and open. DeepSeek's reasoning models and Alibaba's Qwen family are downloadable, widely fine-tuned and competitive at the mid-tier. That has already changed pricing outside China, because a free base model is a ceiling on what an API can charge for commodity work.

The accelerator layer is real but constrained. Huawei's Ascend 910C is manufactured on SMIC's 7nm-class N+2 process, achieved with deep-ultraviolet multi-patterning rather than extreme ultraviolet. The chips work; they are more expensive per unit of performance and harder to cool. Huawei's answer is system design: CloudMatrix 384 links 384 chips to reach rack-scale throughput by spending more silicon and more power.

The energy layer is the quiet advantage. East Data, West Computing moved capacity toward cheap western power, and China leads on ultra-high-voltage transmission and on fusion pulse duration, with EAST holding the 1,066-second record set in January 2025. Cheap, dispatchable electricity is the input AI clusters actually need.

## What is announced

Domestic high-bandwidth memory, a fully domestic EUV toolchain and humanoid robots at industrial scale are all targets, not deliveries. Each has credible engineering behind it and each is routinely described as further along than the public evidence supports.

## The policy layer

The 15th Five-Year Plan, adopted in outline in October 2025, puts self-reliance in science and technology at the centre of the 2026–2030 period. Big Fund III, capitalised at RMB 344bn in 2024, is directed at equipment and materials rather than fabs. Neither is a budget guarantee, but together they tell you where subsidised capacity will appear, which is usually where prices fall fastest.

## What would change this view

Three falsifiers: verified domestic HBM in volume, a leading-edge yield improvement without EUV, and evidence that subsidised humanoid deployments earn a return rather than accumulating in warehouses. Until then, treat Chinese capability claims as engineering programmes with real milestones and real gaps, not as a single number on a benchmark chart.

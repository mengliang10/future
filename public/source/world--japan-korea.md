
# Japan and Korea

The AI narrative is written around model companies and GPU designers, but the constraints sit one layer down: memory bandwidth, lithography, etch, dicing and packaging. Japan and Korea dominate that layer, which makes them indispensable even where they have no consumer platform.

## Korea: memory is the bottleneck

SK hynix turned high-bandwidth memory from a commodity into a gate. Its HBM stacks are the reason accelerators can be fed at all, and HBM4 — a wider interface, more dies, custom base layers — extends that position into the next platform generation. Samsung is the second source, and its SF2 gate-all-around node is a test of whether it can win foundry customers who want an alternative to TSMC.

Korea's weakness is concentration. Memory is cyclical, and a capacity build that arrives during a pause in AI orders would hit both companies at once. NAVER's HyperCLOVA X is the country's attempt at sovereign AI, and it is a procurement argument — Korean-language quality and jurisdiction — rather than a benchmark contest.

## Japan: the tools and the materials

Japan's position is in equipment and materials: Tokyo Electron in deposition, etch and coat-develop, Disco in grinding and dicing, Fanuc in factory automation. Cryogenic etching is a good example of why this matters: 3D NAND scaling now happens vertically, and reaching more than 400 layers is an etch problem before it is a lithography problem.

Rapidus is the exception — a state-backed attempt to return to leading-edge logic at 2nm, with a pilot line in Hokkaido and a 2027 production target. It is a genuine industrial-policy bet, and yield learning at that speed has no precedent for a new entrant.

In space, Japan has two commercially interesting niches: ispace's lunar delivery service, which is a reliability business where one crash consumes years of funding, and Astroscale's debris inspection, where the paying customer is a regulator or insurer rather than a satellite owner.

## What would change this view

HBM4 qualification slipping, Rapidus failing to convert pilot yield into customer commitments, or a memory glut arriving alongside an AI order pause. Any of those would reprice the component layer quickly, because it is more cyclical than the model layer it serves.

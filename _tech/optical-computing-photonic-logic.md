Optical computing involves using photons instead of electrons to perform logic operations and arithmetic. Unlike silicon photonics interconnects (which move data), optical computing aims to *process* data entirely in the light domain. This technology promises to bypass the "power wall" of electronic semiconductors, offering orders of magnitude higher speed and lower energy consumption.

Key attributes:
- **Zero Resistive Heating:** Photons do not generate heat through resistance, allowing for extremely dense integration.
- **Speed of Light Processing:** Logic operations performed at the fundamental limit of speed.
- **Parallelism:** Ability to process multiple wavelengths (colors) of light simultaneously through a single logic gate (WDM).
- **Prerequisites:** High-efficiency photonic transistors, non-linear optical materials, and integrated laser sources.

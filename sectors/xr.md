## Why This Sector Matters

Spatial computing replaces the flat screen with an immersive overlay on physical reality. Every interface — work, social, gaming, navigation — gets redesigned for a 3D layer. The smartphone market took 10 years from iPhone launch to ubiquity; AR glasses will be faster because the enabling AI, display, and chip infrastructure already exists. The hardware timeline is: 600g VR headset (current) → 100g mixed reality → 30g AR glasses (2030–2032). At 30g and $300, this is a mass-market consumer product. The investment case today is in the infrastructure that will power that transition: GPU compute, spatial AI, and the content platforms that will have millions of users before the glasses arrive.

## Causal Signals

- **Hardware price and weight** are the adoption gating factors. When standalone AR glasses hit 100g and $500, the addressable market jumps from millions to billions. Track Meta, Apple, and Qualcomm's annual hardware spec announcements.
- **Developer ecosystem growth** — Roblox creator count, Meta Horizon developer MAUs, and Apple Vision Pro app approvals are the leading indicators of content depth that drives consumer adoption. Content follows hardware; adoption follows content.
- **Enterprise XR adoption** — Boeing, Airbus, and automotive OEMs are deploying AR-guided manufacturing. Enterprise contract announcements from PTC, Siemens, and Microsoft HoloLens are the near-term revenue signal that proves industrial use cases before consumer scale.
- **AI spatial rendering** — NVIDIA's DLSS and Omniverse, Apple's spatial AI, and Meta's AI-generated environments reduce the compute cost of immersive graphics. Each generation of GPU makes XR more accessible; track annual GPU architecture releases.
